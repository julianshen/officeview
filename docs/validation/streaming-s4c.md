## Final review fixes (d469bb62, 811c3835, d0d4a4a7)

The whole-branch review (`.superpowers/sdd/2026-11-26-streaming-s4c/final-review.md`) found three Important defects. Each fix has a regression written from the reviewer's reproduction.
- **R3: queued jobs reran a refused prep (d469bb62).** `Pool.sheetView`, `sheetText` and `tile` check the remembered prep verdict again once they hold the worker's exclusive slot, before reopening or preparing. The early check stays. Test: `server/test/sheet-view-prep.test.ts` `describe('jobs queued before a prep refusal (final review R3)')`: a derive, find or tile queued beside a derive whose prep times out gets `SheetPrepRefused`, with one prep and one retirement. RED: 2 preps, 2 retirements in all three.
- **R1: the display-text merge ran after the raw distinct-value cap (811c3835).** The merge now reads every local value through an additive enumerator: `ValueIndex.universePage` and the optional `ValuesDraftPort.universe`, page-bounded, charged and settled like a read page. So `VALUE_DISPLAY_LIMIT` caps display entries, and `truncated` means an unlisted display text exists. A values rule selects every local value painting a chosen entry's text; a value of an unlisted entry follows the rule's mode. The local draft takes the form (base none or all) needing fewer toggles. When both need more than `VALUES_RULE_FLIPS` (= `VALUE_DISPLAY_LIMIT`, the most toggles a rule could take before), the filter is refused `view-refused {reason: 'resource-limit'}`, never committed wrong. A search maps its matches through the same grouping. The universe scan is bounded by the local value index's memory quota: a 400,000-distinct column already fails the index build, and 200,000 distinct values list in about 0.27 s and filter in 0.25 s (macOS, measured). The local dropdown lists raw entries and does not group by display text, so the local engine does not have this bug and its behaviour is unchanged. Test: `server/test/sheet-view-values-groups.test.ts`, on the reviewer's 10,002-row `0`-format fixture: three entries, `truncated: false`; include and exclude forms of four selections give equal masks and counts and the exact painted membership; a search past the raw cap; the cap on display groups; the refusal. RED: `truncated: true` on the reviewer's fixture.
- **R2: cached derived views outlived their hold (d0d4a4a7).** The socket server binds its `DocumentLedger` holds to the pool (`Pool.retainViewsBy`; `DocumentLedger.isHeld`). When a view's last hold goes (replacement, clear, its session closing while another keeps the document), the pool queues `sheet-view-release` on the document's worker, FIFO behind the jobs already waiting there. It drops the `derivedViews` entry, the sized geometries and text views over the view, and the port's state when it is that view (`SheetViewPort.forget`). A sort or filter's next view is held from before its pool job until the op ends, so a cancelled or refused commit drops it. After every tile, text and `sheet-view` job, in the same slot turn, the pool releases any view the job may have cached that no hold charges. The identity view is no longer put in the derived cache. Test: `server/test/sheet-view-retained.test.ts`, through the real socket server and Pool, with a TEST-ONLY retained-views probe: 8 sorts with a tile each keep exactly one derived view; a clear leaves nothing and the ledger is at its baseline; the last sorted holder closing while a third session stays drops it; a sort cancelled after its job ran leaves nothing. RED by mutation (the binding and the release removed): all three fail, 2 views and 2 geometries retained, or ~128 KiB left after the ledger was back at baseline.
  - **The remaining window:** the ledger releases a hold at once and the release message follows it. Until that turn runs, the worker still holds the view's bytes uncharged; the window is bounded by the jobs already queued on that worker. Releasing the bytes only on the worker's acknowledgement would make the existing million-row test (`sheet-sort-filter` "a million-row sort", which reads the ledger right after the last close) racy, so the immediate release was kept.

## Prep deadline (Task 14a)

Owner ruling, 2026-11-28: preparing a sheet's sort data is split from the sort or filter, as two pool jobs. Before each `sheet-view` job that reads the sheet's port, `Pool.sheetView` posts `sheet-view-prep`, which builds the snapshot, the key view and the port. The jobs that read the port are sort, filter, derive, value lists, detection, and an extent under a non-identity view. Both jobs share one admission charge, one hold of the worker slot and one `'sheet'` request. A warm port answers the prep at once and builds nothing.
- **The prep deadline** is `renderDeadlineMs + PREP_MS_PER_MIB × admitted MiB`, at most `PREP_DEADLINE_CAP_MS`. `PREP_MS_PER_MIB` is 1,000 ms. `PREP_DEADLINE_CAP_MS` is 30,000 ms, the parse's own default deadline. "Admitted MiB" means the document's bytes as the document ledger admitted them.
- **The op keeps the 2 s render deadline.** `renderDeadlineMs` and every other limit are unchanged.
- **A prep timeout or failure** answers `refused {code: 'view-refused', reason: 'too-large'}`, with a `view-refused` row where the op writes one. The client shows the local `resource-limit` refusal, "This sheet is too large to sort or filter in this viewing mode."
- **A cancel during prep** runs no op.
- **Fix round 1 (9985539c, controller rulings).**
  - **Tile and sheet-text jobs prep too.** A tile or a find, copy, a11y or fit job under a non-identity view now runs the same prep first, under the same deadline. That covers a cold port after a worker recycle. A prep refusal answers find, copy, a11y and fit with `view-refused {reason: 'too-large'}`, and the client shows the same local refusal. A tile batch gets the same refusal when its extent is refused. Otherwise each refused tile gets `{col, row, code: 'view-refused', reason: 'too-large'}`.
  - **The verdict is remembered.** It is kept per `(docRef, policy, sheetIndex, clock)` while the document is registered, and cleared on re-register and forget. A later job that reads that port is refused at once, with no admission and no worker. So the client's re-fetch after a refusal retires no second worker. `Pool.stats().sheetPreps` counts the prep jobs posted.
  - **Owner decision, pre-PR (1f6c2fd0): a remembered timeout expires.** A `'timeout'` verdict is refused for `PREP_TIMEOUT_MEMORY_MS` (60,000 ms, on the pool's clock) only; past that it is dropped and the next job preps again, and a new timeout re-arms it. A `'failed'` verdict is kept for the document's life. Tests: `server/test/sheet-view-prep.test.ts` "a job within 60 s of a prep timeout is refused too-large with no new prep and no new retire", "past 60 s the remembered timeout is dropped: the same job preps again and commits", "a new timeout re-arms the 60 s", "a failed prep is still refused past 60 s" (RED before the fix: 2 fail).
  - **Deviation from the ruling's key: the canonical view is left out.** The prep builds the port of `(sheet, clock)`, whatever the view. The re-fetch derives a different view (the current one) than the refused sort, so a view in the key would prep again. The ruling's re-fetch test would then fail.

**Measurements behind the constants.** All are on `oneMillionSort`: 7.6 MiB admitted, 1,048,576 rows. Each run was a separate process with a one-worker pool, timing the worker's `sheet-view-prep` with the snapshot already built by `sheet-layout`.

| Run | `'full'` prep | `visible-only` prep |
|---|---|---|
| quiet, 3 runs (load ≈ 4.7) | 546–553 ms | 1,901–2,052 ms |
| beside the server sheet suites, 2 runs (load 5.2–5.6) | 654–954 ms | 1,585–1,977 ms |

- A cold snapshot adds 338–587 ms; after a worker recycle the prep builds the snapshot too.
- The sort after the prep took 720–978 ms (under the 2 s deadline).
- In-process, the cold sort without the split took 1,304 ms under `'full'` and 2,901 ms under `visible-only`, and the pool's `visible-only` cold sort threw `RenderTimeout` at 2,597 ms.
- The deadline for this document is 2,000 + 7.6 × 1,000 ≈ 9.6 s. That is 3.7× the slowest prep plus a cold snapshot (about 2.6 s), against the ≥ 2× the ruling asked for.
